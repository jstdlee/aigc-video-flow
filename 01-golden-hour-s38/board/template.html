Film styling

1997, shot on 35mm

Late-90s spy thriller

The story is set in 1997, the end of the Cold War hangover. Eli's 1970s muscle car is old-school grit; Kaz's brand-new 1990s JDM coupe is street-racer swagger. Traffic, trucks and sedans are 1990s models.

  • 35mm Kodak film stock: fine visible grain, gentle halation, softer contrast, creamy highlights.
  • Anamorphic lenses, oval flare, 2.39:1 framing; practical, real-speed camera-car feel.
  • Motion: real-time 24 fps, 180° shutter. Slow motion only in shots 5–6 (the stare and the shout).

Light: one key for the whole scene

  • Low golden-hour sun ahead of the cars, 3200 K warm key.
  • 6500 K sky fill; warm rim light on every car and face.
  • Haze in the hills; long shadows fall back toward camera.

Colour ratio 60 : 30 : 10

60 asphalt + ochre 30 olive-teal + pine 10 red

Reference cues

  • Mood: late-90s practical car-chase cinema: real cars at real speed, smoky dusk, grain you can feel.
  • Cutting: hard cuts on action; inserts (the lid, the clock, the shifter) carry the stakes; close-ups carry the faces.
  • Avoid: VHS damage, fake scratches, modern digital sharpness, shaky-cam chaos.

Consistency system

How people and places stay the same

Blender owns layout and camera; the asset library owns identity and look. Every keyframe takes both, and passes a written check before it becomes video.

People stay consistent because…

  • Each actor has 5 sheets: full body, faces, seated in his car, faces under the scene light, and a performance close-up sheet (Kaz’s rage, Eli’s reaction).
  • Seated poses come from the clay rig, so pose and scale match the shots.
  • Every keyframe names its actor and cites his sheets; the video model gets the same sheets.
  • Rules: Eli always drives car A (left-hand drive); Kaz sits on the far side of car B; nobody leaves a car.

The environment stays consistent because…

  • The Blender lane map is the single truth for lanes, median side and landmark order.
  • Background plates are made from clay frames, so road geometry cannot drift.
  • One grade key still and one light key for all shots; the sun stays ahead.
  • The same 1990s truck and sedan, and one device sheet (countdown, keypad, rust) in every shot that shows them.
  • After the ram (shot 7), every shot of a car uses its damage sheet: car A caved in at the left rear door, car B crushed at the front-right corner.

Sound and dialogue

One line, one pulse

Sound is made after the picture is locked, so it can hit every cut.

Dialogue

“STOP OR DIE!”

  • Who: Kaz, shot 6, shouted across the gap with fury.
  • How: an angry voice from a fal text-to-speech model, timed to the mouth in shot 6. Kaz backs the line with the ram (shots 7–10). Eli answers with no words, only the shifter (shot 14).

Music: spy pursuit

  • Low strings ostinato and a synth-bass pulse from 120 to 140 bpm; taiko and snare hits on cuts.
  • Shot 1: a ticking motif matched to the countdown. Shots 5–6: music drops to a drone. Shot 7: the music cuts out on impact; only metal, tyres and a horn until shot 10. Shot 13: a shrill device alarm over the silence. Shot 14: full hit on the shifter. Shot 16: peak, then cut to silence.
  • Model: Google Lyria 3.5 (DeepMind, Sept 2026), on fal, $0.10 per track. Rated strongest for cinematic, instrumental scores. The prompt carries section timecodes, so the hits land on the cuts. Three takes, best one wins.
  • A/B: ElevenLabs Music, about $0.13 for this length. Suno v5.5 has no official API; for a Suno take, paste the same prompt into the Suno app.
